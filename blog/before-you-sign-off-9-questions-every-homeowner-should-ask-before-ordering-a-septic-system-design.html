Before You Sign Off: 9 Questions Every Homeowner Should Ask Before Ordering a Septic System Design

Designing a septic system is one of those behind-the-scenes projects that quietly determines whether your home runs smoothly for the next 20-30 years or becomes a source of constant headaches. Whether you're building new construction, replacing a failing system, or adding onto your home, the design phase sets the foundation for everything that follows — permitting, installation cost, long-term maintenance, and even your property's resale value.

Many homeowners assume a septic design is a simple form to fill out and submit to the county. In reality, it's a technical process that involves soil science, site conditions, local regulations, and your household's actual water usage. Asking the right questions upfront can save you thousands of dollars and months of delay. Here's what to ask before you order a septic system design.

1. Has a Proper Soil and Site Evaluation Been Completed?

Your septic design is only as good as the data behind it. A qualified evaluator should perform soil borings or test pits to assess soil texture, drainage, seasonal water table depth, and slope. If a designer is willing to skip this step or rely on outdated data from a neighboring property, that's a red flag. Ask specifically how the soil evaluation was conducted and when.

2. What System Type Is Being Recommended, and Why?

Not every property is a good candidate for a standard gravity septic system. Depending on your soil conditions, lot size, and proximity to wells or water bodies, you may need an aerobic treatment unit, a mound system, a sand filter, or a pressure-dosed design. Ask your designer to explain why a specific system type fits your site rather than accepting a generic recommendation.

3. Is the Design Sized for Your Actual Household Needs?

System sizing is based on estimated daily wastewater flow, which is tied to the number of bedrooms, fixtures, and sometimes square footage. If you're planning future additions — a finished basement, an extra bedroom, or a mother-in-law suite — mention it now. Designing for your current needs only, without factoring in realistic future use, often leads to costly system failures or upgrades down the road.

4. What Permits and Approvals Will Be Required?

Septic designs must typically be reviewed and approved by your local health department or environmental agency before installation can begin. Ask who is responsible for submitting the design for permitting, how long approval usually takes in your area, and whether any variances might be needed due to lot size or setback issues.

5. How Will Setbacks and Site Constraints Be Handled?

Septic systems must maintain minimum distances from wells, property lines, water bodies, foundations, and driveways. If your lot is small or oddly shaped, ask early how these constraints will be addressed. It's far better to discover a setback conflict during design than after excavation has already started.

6. What's Included in the Design Package?

A complete septic design should include a site plan, soil evaluation results, system specifications, and installation notes for the contractor. Ask for a clear breakdown of what you're receiving so you know exactly what documentation will be submitted for permitting and handed off to your installer.

7. What Happens If the Design Is Rejected or Needs Revisions?

Permitting agencies sometimes request changes before approval. Ask whether revision work is included in the original design fee or billed separately, and how quickly the designer can turn around requested changes. This helps you avoid surprise costs and unexpected delays later in the process.

8. How Does the Design Account for Long-Term Maintenance?

A well-thought-out design doesn't just get approved — it makes future maintenance easier. Ask whether access risers are included for tank lids, how the system will be inspected over time, and whether the design allows for reasonable pumping access. A little foresight here can significantly reduce maintenance headaches for years to come.

9. What's the Realistic Timeline From Design to Installation?

Between soil testing, design drafting, permitting review, and contractor scheduling, the process from start to finish can take anywhere from a few weeks to a few months depending on your local agency's workload. Ask for a realistic timeline so you can plan construction, closing dates, or move-in schedules accordingly.

Getting the Design Right the First Time

A septic system design isn't just paperwork — it's the blueprint for a system that will manage your household's wastewater for decades. Taking the time to ask these questions upfront helps ensure your system is properly sized, code-compliant, and built to handle your home's real-world needs without unnecessary surprises or costs.
